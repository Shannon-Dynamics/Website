About this book

An interactive book on probabilistic robotics, taught the FCP way — rigorous mathematical Foundations, Conceptual understanding through simulations you can manipulate, and Practical implementations in Rust.

Twenty-six chapters take Rusty, a differential-drive rover, from its first noisy encoder tick to autonomously exploring and mapping a floorplan it has never seen: Bayes filters, Kalman and particle filters, motion and sensor models, localization, occupancy grids, SLAM as sparse least squares, scan matching, visual-inertial odometry, planning under uncertainty, MPPI, POMDPs, and active SLAM.

PART 0

Vision & method

Thrun, Burgard, and Fox's Probabilistic Robotics defined how a generation of roboticists thinks about uncertainty. Twenty years later the field's back end has moved to factor graphs, its filters live on Lie groups, its maps are TSDFs as often as occupancy grids — and no book teaches any of it in Rust, the language the robotics industry is steadily adopting for safety-critical autonomy. This book fills that gap: no interactive book, in any language, teaches probabilistic robotics with in-page simulations, and no serious "robotics algorithms in Rust" text exists at all.

The FCP promise. Foundation — every concept gets its full mathematical formalism: definitions, stated assumptions, and honest derivations, in the notation lineage of Thrun et al. extended to modern on-manifold estimation. Conceptual — every hard idea gets a visual, interactive treatment: dashboards, animations, and web simulations where the reader turns noise parameters, scrubs time, drags robots, and watches beliefs evolve. Practical — every algorithm is implemented in idiomatic Rust inside one cargo workspace, where the book's printed listings, its test suite, its figures, and its in-page WASM demos are literally the same code. Because Rust compiles to WASM, the filter the reader just studied is the filter running in the page — no Python competitor can make that claim.

The running lab. Two worlds thread through the whole book. The Hallway is a 1D corridor with indistinguishable doors — Thrun's classic example, and the pedagogical world where beliefs are plottable as curves. The Apartment is a 2D floorplan with simulated wheel encoders and a 2D LiDAR, ray-cast via parry2d — the realistic world where localization, mapping, SLAM, planning, and the capstone all take place. The book's robot is Rusty, a differential-drive rover built in Chapter 4 that never leaves: by Chapter 26 it autonomously explores and maps an apartment it has never seen.

Reader path per chapter. Hook → Conceptual (play first) → Foundation (now the rigor) → Practical (Rust) → an integration lab that runs it in the Hallway or the Apartment → exercises. Concrete before abstract, always; theorem-proof ordering is used only inside Foundation sections.

Toolchain. The book ships as a Next.js 16 / React 19 static site with Fumadocs for its chrome, math set in KaTeX, code highlighted by Shiki, and dashboards in Nivo. The taught Rust stack runs on nalgebra for small fixed-size linear algebra and faer for sparse graph-SLAM solves; rand/rand_distr/statrs for probability, with every demo seeded for reproducibility; sophus for 3D Lie groups (SE(2) is hand-rolled first, pedagogically); every filter — KF, EKF, UKF, particle — hand-rolled on nalgebra, because that's the point of the book; parry2d for ray-cast LiDAR and collision; petgraph/pathfinding for graphs and search; and candle for the Chapter 25 learned models. Every algorithm is implemented twice, on purpose: the Rust in the text is canonical, and a faithful TypeScript port in web/lib/ powers the in-page simulations, pinned against the Rust by numerical invariants so a reader can compare them line by line.

Baseline references. Thrun, Burgard & Fox's Probabilistic Robotics is the spine of Parts II–VI — note that the source draft is the 1999–2000 sixteen-chapter manuscript, so FastSLAM, published-form GraphSLAM, UKF, KLD-sampling, and exploration are rebuilt from the modern literature. Lynch & Park's Modern Robotics supplies the geometry (SO/SE, exp/log, screws) and the planning baseline; Craig's and Niku's Introduction to Robotics ground the frames and the sensor/actuator physics; Choset et al.'s Principles of Robot Motion supplies the planning taxonomy; Spong et al.'s Robot Modeling and Control cross-references kinematics and control. Where these stop, a modernization set carries the book forward: Dellaert & Kaess on factor graphs, Solà on micro Lie theory, Barfoot on state estimation, KISS-ICP/LOAM, Forster's IMU preintegration, MSCKF/ORB-SLAM3/VINS, OctoMap/Voxblox, Williams' MPPI, POMCP/DESPOT, Placed et al. on active SLAM, and the differentiable-filter and diffusion-policy literature.

PART I — FOUNDATIONS: THE ROBOT AND ITS UNCERTAINTY · CH.01

The Robot That Doubts — Why Probabilistic Robotics?

The five sources of uncertainty; a single best guess vs. a belief; the hallway thought experiment run live; the probabilistic paradigm's implications; tour of the book, the FCP method, and Rusty.

Interactive: the Hallway Belief Machine, in preview — the book's whole thesis in one autoplay animation.

In Rust: Reader environment setup; the workspace skeleton; first cargo run.

PART I — FOUNDATIONS: THE ROBOT AND ITS UNCERTAINTY · CH.02

Probability: The Language of Uncertainty

Random variables, joint/conditional probability, Bayes rule (with background knowledge), independence, expectation, covariance, entropy and information; the Gaussian in 1D and nD, moments vs. canonical form; sampling as representation.

Interactive: the Gaussian Playground — drag μ, stretch Σ, watch iso-ellipses turn into eigenvectors; the Blob Multiplier — Bayes rule as a pointwise product, conjugacy live.

In Rust: rand_distr/statrs/nalgebra: sampling, densities, a MultivariateNormal you understand; seeded reproducibility discipline.

PART I — FOUNDATIONS: THE ROBOT AND ITS UNCERTAINTY · CH.03

The Geometry of Motion: Poses, Frames, and Lie Groups

Frames and rotation matrices; homogeneous transforms; SO(2)/SE(2) fully worked; SO(3)/SE(3), quaternions, axis-angle; exponential/log maps, twists and screws; why "state ⊞ increment" needs ⊞/⊟; uncertainty on manifolds (preview of banana distributions).

Interactive: the Frame Composer — drag frames, compose transforms, watch the leading-superscript notation come alive; the Exp/Log Lens — a twist slider driving a screw-motion animation.

In Rust: nalgebra Isometry2/3, UnitQuaternion; hand-rolled SE2 type with exp/log/adjoint; sophus for 3D; compile-time frame-safety via newtypes.

PART I — FOUNDATIONS: THE ROBOT AND ITS UNCERTAINTY · CH.04

Rusty, Sensors, and the Simulator

Differential-drive kinematics and wheel odometry from encoder ticks; sensor taxonomy grounded in real devices (encoders, IMU, sonar, LiDAR physics, cameras); noise phenomenology measured from the simulator; building the book's lab: the Hallway and Apartment worlds, ray-cast LiDAR via parry2d, fixed-seed determinism; anatomy of a book widget (eframe + WASM).

Interactive: Rusty's Dashboard — drive Rusty with the arrow keys and watch the encoders drift from ground truth; LiDAR Anatomy — beam-by-beam ray casting with noise injection.

In Rust: sim crate: World, Robot, Lidar, Encoders; the widget framework skeleton every later chapter reuses.

PART II — THE BAYES FILTER FAMILY · CH.05

The Bayes Filter: Recursive State Estimation

States, completeness, and the Markov assumption; controls vs. measurements as two data streams; beliefs; the Bayes filter recursion derived by induction; "sensing sharpens, moving smears"; representation/computation trade-offs — the family tree of every filter to come.

Interactive: the full Hallway Belief Machine — scrub time, toggle sense/move, break the Markov assumption on purpose.

In Rust: trait BayesFilter { fn predict(&mut self, u); fn correct(&mut self, z); } — the trait the whole book implements; discrete hallway filter as first impl; unit test reproduces the chapter's 3-step numeric worked example.

PART II — THE BAYES FILTER FAMILY · CH.06

Kalman Filters: The Linear-Gaussian World

Linear-Gaussian systems; the KF derived by completing the square; the Kalman gain as precision- weighted trust; the information filter and canonical form duality; matrix inversion lemma; smoothing (RTS) as the first taste of "whole-trajectory" estimation.

Interactive: the Kalman Tuning Bench — a 1D cart with sliders for R and Q, color-coded prior/prediction/measurement/posterior, divergence on purpose; Moments vs. Information — the same belief in two parameterizations, the cost of each operation shown live.

In Rust: Kf on nalgebra SMatrix; RTS smoother; cross-validation vs adskalman; 1D + 2D tracking labs.

PART II — THE BAYES FILTER FAMILY · CH.07

Beyond Linearity: EKF, UKF, and Estimation on Manifolds

Taylor linearization and its lies; the EKF; the unscented transform and UKF; where 2005 stops and 2026 begins: error-state formulation, ⊞/⊟ retractions, EKF/UKF on SE(2)/SE(3), invariant-EKF intuition; when each filter breaks, demonstrated.

Interactive: the Linearization Lens — a nonlinear function plus a Gaussian in, the true pushforward vs. EKF vs. UKF sigma points, error updating live as you slide the operating point; Manifold vs. Vector — the heading wrap-around catastrophe, fixed by an on-manifold update.

In Rust: generic Ekf/Ukf over a Manifold trait; error-state EKF on our SE2; deliberate compile-error showing the type system rejecting a dimension mismatch.

PART II — THE BAYES FILTER FAMILY · CH.08

Nonparametric Filters: Histograms and Particles

Discrete Bayes filter; grid decompositions; binary static-state filter in log odds (the occupancy seed); importance sampling from first principles; the particle filter; resampling variance, low- variance/systematic resampling, particle deprivation; KLD-adaptive sample sizes.

Interactive: the Particle Survival Arena — watch weights, then watch resampling kill and clone particles; the Resampling Wheel — roulette vs. low-variance comb, side by side, variance measured live.

In Rust: ParticleFilter<S> with pluggable proposal/likelihood; low-variance resampler in 15 lines; rayon-parallel weights (native) with the same code single-threaded on WASM.

PART III — PROBABILISTIC MODELS · CH.09

Probabilistic Motion Models

Probabilistic kinematics; the velocity model (closed form + sampler, α₁…α₆) derived from arc geometry; the odometry model (δ_rot1, δ_trans, δ_rot2); the banana distribution as truth vs. its Gaussian caricature; map-conditioned motion; on-manifold noise (exponential-coordinate perturbations) as the modern formulation.

Interactive: the Banana Machine — drive a command, sample 1,000 futures, sliders for each α; the Gaussian overlay shows exactly what EKFs get wrong.

In Rust: motion module: both models, both variants, property-tested (samples ↔ closed form via KS test); the samplers that power every localization demo to come.

PART III — PROBABILISTIC MODELS · CH.10

Probabilistic Sensor Models

The beam model as a four-way mixture (hit/short/max/rand) with EM fitting of intrinsics; likelihood fields (and their smoothness advantage); map correlation; feature/landmark models with range-bearing-signature and the correspondence variable c_t; over-confidence from independence assumptions and its mitigations.

Interactive: the Beam Mixture Mixer — four sliders reshape the density against real simulated beams; the Likelihood Field Explorer — drag Rusty and watch per-beam likelihood and the field itself as a heatmap.

In Rust: sensor module: BeamModel (with learn_intrinsics), LikelihoodField (precomputed distance transform), LandmarkModel; benchmarked against each other in the Apartment.

PART IV — LOCALIZATION · CH.11

Localization I: Tracking with Gaussians

The localization taxonomy (tracking / global / kidnapped; static / dynamic; passive / active); Markov localization as the Bayes filter with a map; EKF localization with known then unknown correspondences; maximum-likelihood data association, Mahalanobis gating, validation regions; multi-hypothesis tracking as a Gaussian-mixture stopgap.

Interactive: the Association Gate — drag a measurement around a landmark field, gates drawn as ellipses, watch a wrong association poison the filter; the EKF Localization Lab — a landmark world with uncertainty ellipses breathing as landmarks come into view.

In Rust: EkfLocalizer over the Ch. 7 error-state EKF + Ch. 9/10 models; MHT with pruning as an optional feature flag.

PART IV — LOCALIZATION · CH.12

Localization II: Global Localization with Grids and Particles

Grid localization (resolution/precision trade, likelihood pre-caching); Monte Carlo Localization as the particle filter instantiated; Augmented MCL (w_fast/w_slow random-particle recovery) solving the kidnapped-robot problem; dual/mixture proposals; localization among moving people (measurement novelty filtering); the great comparison table, reproduced experimentally.

Interactive: the MCL Theater — the book's centerpiece: global localization in the Apartment, the particle cloud condensing from everywhere to one hypothesis, a kidnap button, symmetry ambiguity on display.

In Rust: Mcl + AugmentedMcl (AMCL — still Nav2's default localizer — built by hand); side-by-side benchmark: EKF vs grid vs MCL on identical logs; this chapter's demo is the book's public demo.

PART V — MAPPING AND SLAM · CH.13

Occupancy Grid Mapping

Mapping with known poses; per-cell binary Bayes filters in log odds; inverse sensor models (hand-crafted, then learned from the forward model); multi-sensor fusion; where per-cell independence lies to you, and MAP mapping with forward models as the honest alternative.

Interactive: the Map Weaver — drive Rusty and watch the map grow in log odds, gray meaning ignorance; the Independence Trap — the conflict-in-doorways artifact, then a MAP repair.

In Rust: OccGrid with the Ch. 8 binary filter per cell; Bresenham ray updates; live map streaming into egui at 60 fps.

PART V — MAPPING AND SLAM · CH.14

The SLAM Problem and EKF SLAM

Online vs. full SLAM; the joint pose-map state; why correlations are the map's value; EKF SLAM with known then unknown correspondence; provisional landmarks and map management; the two fatal flaws — quadratic cost and linearization-locked inconsistency — that motivate everything after.

Interactive: the Correlation Web — landmarks linked by correlation strength, loop closure snapping the whole web tight, with a linked covariance-matrix heatmap; the Consistency Autopsy — watch EKF SLAM go overconfident and wrong on a long loop.

In Rust: EkfSlam with dynamic state growth; instrumented to export the covariance movie the widgets replay.

PART V — MAPPING AND SLAM · CH.15

SLAM as Least Squares: Factor Graphs

The modern backbone. Full-SLAM posterior in log form = sum of quadratic constraints = a factor graph; MAP inference as sparse nonlinear least squares; Gauss-Newton and Levenberg-Marquardt; sparsity, variable elimination, and the Schur complement (the EIF "reduce" step, reborn); robust kernels and outlier-immune SLAM; the information-form lineage (EIF/SEIF) told honestly as history whose sparsity insight won via smoothing.

Interactive: the Spring-Graph Optimizer — poses as nodes, factors as springs, energy relaxation running live; add a bad loop factor, then rescue it with a robust kernel; the Sparsity Scope — information-matrix fill-in as the elimination order changes.

In Rust: hand-rolled dense GN/LM on nalgebra → sparse via faer; then the same graph in factrs 0.3 (GTSAM-style typed factors) to show the production path.

PART V — MAPPING AND SLAM · CH.16

Scan Matching and Pose-Graph SLAM

Front-end/back-end architecture; ICP (point-to-point, point-to-plane) derived and implemented; NDT; scan-to-map matching; KISS-ICP-style odometry; loop-closure detection and verification; pose-graph SLAM end-to-end — the Cartographer/SLAM-Toolbox recipe, built by hand.

Interactive: the ICP Stepper — correspondences → transform → iterate, scrub each iteration by hand; Loop Snap — drift accumulates around the Apartment, loop closure fires, and the rubber-band correction propagates back through the trajectory.

In Rust: RustSLAM-2D: LiDAR odometry (ICP over parry-simulated scans) + pose graph on our Ch. 15 optimizer + occupancy submaps = the book's first complete SLAM system.

PART V — MAPPING AND SLAM · CH.17

FastSLAM and Rao-Blackwellization

Factoring the SLAM posterior: sample paths, close maps in closed form; Rao-Blackwellization as a theorem, not a trick; FastSLAM 1.0/2.0 with per-particle landmark EKFs and tree-shared maps; grid-based RBPF (the gmapping recipe) with improved proposals; when particle SLAM still wins.

Interactive: Parallel Universes — each particle drags its own private map; resampling picks which universe survives; loop closure is universe selection, made visible.

In Rust: FastSlam (landmark version) + grid-RBPF on the Ch. 8 particle machinery; rayon-parallel per-particle maps.

PART V — MAPPING AND SLAM · CH.18

Visual and Visual-Inertial SLAM

The camera as a probabilistic sensor: projection factors and reprojection error; two-view geometry in 90 seconds; IMU preintegration as a factor (the Lupton/Forster idea); MSCKF vs. sliding-window smoothing; marginalization and its fill-in (SEIF's lesson resurfacing); ORB-SLAM3 and VINS as systems — architecture described, not dissected.

Interactive: the Reprojection Playground — drag a 3D point or a camera pose and watch residuals update live; the Preintegration Timeline — IMU samples compressing into one factor between keyframes.

In Rust: bundle-adjust a tiny synthetic scene with our Ch. 15 optimizer + sophus SE(3); factor graph with preintegrated IMU factors in factrs; deliberately smaller P section.

PART V — MAPPING AND SLAM · CH.19

Modern Map Representations

Beyond flat grids: octrees/OctoMap; TSDF and ESDF fusion as recursive estimation (per-voxel weighted least squares — the Bayes filter in disguise); meshes; distance fields for planning; one principled section on differentiable rendering as a measurement model (NeRF/3DGS mapping) with survey pointers, not system worship.

Interactive: the TSDF Sculptor — watch depth scans carve a signed-distance field, with a weight slider; the Representation Gallery — the same Apartment as a grid, an octree, a TSDF, and a mesh, memory and query cost measured side by side.

In Rust: 2D TSDF fusion + ESDF via distance transform; three-d for the one 3D showpiece.

PART VI — PLANNING AND ACTING UNDER UNCERTAINTY · CH.20

Motion Planning: From Geometry to Probability

Configuration space (via Lynch/Choset); graph search done right (Dijkstra, A, D Lite mention); potential fields and their local minima; sampling-based planning: PRM, RRT, RRT with probabilistic completeness and asymptotic optimality stated precisely; planning on Rusty's real constraints (nonholonomic RRT variants, Dubins/Reeds-Shepp primitives, hybrid-A sketch).

Interactive: the Planner Arena — same map, same query: A* on a lattice vs. PRM vs. RRT vs. RRT*, race mode plus a tree-growth animation; the Narrow Passage — why sampling struggles, with a visibility intuition.

In Rust: planning module on petgraph/pathfinding; RRT* from scratch with parry2d collision; Dubins steering for Rusty.

PART VI — PLANNING AND ACTING UNDER UNCERTAINTY · CH.21

Decision Making I: MDPs and Value Iteration

From plans to policies; rewards, discounting, the Bellman equation; value iteration and policy extraction; stochastic shortest paths; the gridworld that refuses to walk straight; policy iteration and asynchronous variants; the bridge: what if state itself is uncertain?

Interactive: the Policy Painter — edit rewards and noise, watch the value function as a heatmap and the policy as arrows re-converge live.

In Rust: mdp module: value/policy iteration over sparse transition models; Rusty navigating with slippery wheels.

PART VI — PLANNING AND ACTING UNDER UNCERTAINTY · CH.22

Decision Making II: POMDPs and Belief-Space Planning

The belief MDP; the tiger problem played honestly; α-vectors and piecewise-linear-convex value functions; exact value iteration and why it explodes; point-based methods (PBVI); modern online solvers — POMCP and DESPOT — as the practical path; AMDP/coastal navigation (uncertainty- aware behavior emerging from planning in belief space).

Interactive: the Tiger Door Console — play the POMDP yourself, then watch the optimal policy, a belief segment with the α-vector envelope underneath; the Coastal Navigator — why the optimal path hugs the wall.

In Rust: exact finite-world POMDP solver; POMCP over the book's particle machinery; Rusty choosing to relocalize before committing to a corridor.

PART VI — PLANNING AND ACTING UNDER UNCERTAINTY · CH.23

Stochastic Model Predictive Control: MPPI and Friends

Receding-horizon control; DWA as the classical baseline; the path-integral idea: rollouts, costs, exponential weighting — MPPI as importance sampling over controls (the particle filter's twin); constraint handling; when MPPI beats gradient MPC and vice versa.

Interactive: the Rollout Storm — hundreds of candidate trajectories, cost-colored, collapsing into the chosen control every frame.

In Rust: Mppi with rayon rollouts (single-thread on WASM, still real-time for 2D Rusty); obstacle costs from the Ch. 19 ESDF; Rusty tracking a path through clutter.

PART VI — PLANNING AND ACTING UNDER UNCERTAINTY · CH.24

Exploration and Active SLAM

Where should Rusty go to learn? Information gain and expected entropy reduction; frontier exploration; active localization (choosing motions that disambiguate); active SLAM as decision- making over the Ch. 15 graph (utility = coverage + expected information); stopping criteria; the Placed et al. survey as the map of the field.

Interactive: the Frontier Chaser — frontiers highlighted, utilities scored, a target chosen while the map's entropy curve falls; Disambiguation Detour — active localization choosing the informative corridor over the short one.

In Rust: frontier detector + information-gain scorer over OccGrid; greedy explorer; hooks the Ch. 16 SLAM stack — Rusty maps a floorplan it has never seen, autonomously.

PART VII — FRONTIERS AND INTEGRATION · CH.25

Learning in the Loop

Machine learning inside the Bayesian frame, not instead of it: learned and calibrated observation models (the Ch. 13 learned-inverse-model idea, modernized); calibration curves; differentiable filters (backprop through the KF/PF); diffusion policies as probabilistic action models; what stays principled and what is still alchemy.

Interactive: the Calibration Clinic — an overconfident vs. a calibrated sensor model, a reliability diagram live; the Differentiable Filter Trainer — watch the loss fall as the learned noise model adapts.

In Rust: candle: train a small learned beam model from simulator data; differentiate through the Ch. 6 KF; honest benchmarks vs. the hand-tuned models.

PART VII — FRONTIERS AND INTEGRATION · CH.26

Capstone: A Complete Autonomous Robot

Everything, integrated and honest: architecture of an autonomy stack (estimation, mapping, planning, control as concurrent tasks); Rusty dropped into an unknown apartment — explores (Ch. 24), SLAMs (Ch. 16), plans (Ch. 20), controls (Ch. 23), replans on surprise; failure modes tour (kidnaps, dynamic obstacles, sensor dropout) with the probabilistic machinery recovering; engineering retrospective: what Rust's type system caught, what it cost, where the ecosystem stands; where to go next (ROS 2 via ros2-rust, real hardware, 3D).

Interactive: the Grand Demo — a full-page finale: autonomous exploration of a randomized apartment, every subsystem's internals inspectable — belief, map, graph, frontier scores, MPPI rollouts.

In Rust: capstone crate wiring every module of the workspace; the book's closing argument that the whole stack — teaching code included — runs at real-time in the browser.
